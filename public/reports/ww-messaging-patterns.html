Repetition

Both sides run on a template

The most repeated lines are not the sharpest ones. They are the boilerplate.

Website · one sentence, two dozen pages

"deploys donated capital" ×24 "produces documentary films" ×24 "cooperatives, mutual banks" ×24 "Partner through donations, co-investment, press" ×4

Roughly a third of all pages carry this identical description. It is a clear, strong sentence, which is exactly why repeating it verbatim wastes it.

What the patterns say

Where the language could pull together

  1. The reason-why is trapped on the website
    Mutual banks, land trusts, capital that stays local, ownership by members "not investors": zero of these reach Instagram. The most distinctive part of the story is the part social never tells.
  2. The strongest line is buried in boilerplate
    "Deploys donated capital into cooperatives, mutual banks, and community land trusts, and produces documentary films" is the clearest articulation of the model, yet it sits in 24 duplicate meta descriptions instead of leading the hero and the social bio.
  3. Two voices that could lend each other strength
    The web has the substance but files it as a description; Instagram has the warmth (109 "we/our") but little substance. The site could borrow the first person; the feed could borrow the specifics.
  4. The question is the shared asset
    Both channels already ask questions (19 on IG, 16 on the web). It is the one device that travels, and the natural bridge for turning a web explainer into a scroll-stopping caption.
  5. Vary the frame, not just the hook
    The podcast-promo template and the repeated meta sentence both flatten the message through repetition. The contrast device the site uses well, "owned by members, not outside investors," is a sharper, more portable frame than "link in bio."
